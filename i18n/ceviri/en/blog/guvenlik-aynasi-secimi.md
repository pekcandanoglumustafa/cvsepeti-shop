A badly positioned safety mirror is worse than none at all — the driver trusts the mirror and assumes a vehicle he cannot see is not there. This article explains the right diameter, angle and position.

## What does a safety mirror do?

Thanks to its convex surface, it shows a wide area at a single glance. It reflects to the observer an area that cannot be seen because of a wall, column or corner.

In return, it reduces the image size and makes distances appear greater than they are. This is the mirror's natural limitation — which is why matching diameter to distance is critical.

## What size mirror do you need?

As the diameter increases, both the field of view and the effective distance increase.

| Diameter | Effective distance | Use |
|---|---|---|
| 40 cm | 5–8 m | Narrow corridors, between warehouse racks |
| 60 cm | 10–15 m | Car park turns, junctions within residential complexes |
| 80 cm | 15–20 m | Ramp exits, large car parks, factory roads |
| 100 cm | 20–30 m | Road junctions, main facility entrances |

The distance is measured from the mirror to the observer's position. Beyond this distance, the image becomes too small to read.

If in doubt, choose the next size up — a small mirror is useless, while a large mirror is only slightly more expensive.

## At what height is the mirror mounted?

A height of **2.2 – 2.5 metres** above the ground is standard.

The reason for this range: it must be high enough that parked vehicles and pedestrians do not block the view, but not so high that it falls outside the driver's line of sight.

In indoor car parks with a low ceiling, the mirror is placed close to the ceiling and its tilt is increased.

## How is the mirror angle adjusted?

The mirror should face the observer's line of sight at an angle of approximately **30-45 degrees**.

A practical adjustment method: fit the mirror, drive to the point where the observer will be, and look into the mirror. The area you want to see should be in the **centre** of the mirror, not at its edge. The image at the edge is distorted.

Working with two people is most efficient — one adjusts the mirror while the other directs from the driver's position.

## Where should it be positioned?

There are five critical points:

**Ramp exits** — in an indoor car park, a vehicle coming up a ramp cannot see the horizontal roadway. The mirror is placed at the ramp exit, on the opposite wall.

**Sharp turns** — on 90-degree turns, oncoming vehicles cannot be seen. The mirror is placed at the outer corner of the turn.

**T-junctions** — when pulling out onto a main road, both directions need to be seen at once. A single mirror is not enough here; either two mirrors or a two-way panoramic mirror is used.

**Warehouse aisles** — placed at aisle intersections where there is forklift traffic.

**Building exits** — in front of doors where pedestrians step out onto the roadway.

## Acrylic or polycarbonate?

**Acrylic** is cheaper, offers high image clarity and lasts a long time indoors. However, it is brittle under impact and yellows over time outdoors due to UV exposure.

**Polycarbonate** is much more impact-resistant, does not break and withstands outdoor conditions. Its image clarity is slightly lower than acrylic and it is more expensive.

There are also **stainless steel** mirrors — used where the risk of vandalism is high and in harsh environments such as tunnels.

The decision is simple: enclosed and sheltered areas, acrylic; open areas with impact risk, polycarbonate.

## Why is the frame orange or yellow?

High-visibility colours make the mirror itself noticeable. The driver sees the frame first and then looks into the mirror.

The frame also protects the mirror from impact and acts as a visor that keeps rainwater from running onto the surface.

## Is maintenance needed?

A dirty mirror surface loses its function. Cleaning once a month outdoors and once every three months indoors is sufficient.

Use a soft cloth and soapy water. Abrasive cleaners and stiff brushes scratch the surface, and scratches permanently impair the image.

The tightness of the mounting clamps should be checked once a year — a loose mirror gradually loses its angle and nobody notices.
