Two different people searching for "safety barrier" are looking for completely different products. One wants to install an automatic boom barrier at a car park entrance; the other is looking for a portable barrier to fence off roadworks. This article is about the second.

## What is a safety barrier?

A portable safety barrier is a modular element that physically delimits an area and visually signals that entry is prohibited. It is not fixed and can be set up and packed away.

It differs from the boom barriers at car park entrances — those are electric access control systems and are not the subject of this article.

## How many types of safety barrier are there?

There are four main types, and each does a different job.

| Type | Material | Weight | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plastic road barrier | PPC / PE | 8–20 kg | Roadworks, lane closures |
| Metal pedestrian barricade | Tube profile | 12–18 kg | Crowd management, checkpoints |
| Water-filled barrier | PE, hollow | 12 kg empty / 250 kg full | Impact absorption, long-term works |
| Folding barrier | PPC + sign panel | 10–15 kg | Temporary, frequently moved applications |

## When is a plastic road barrier used?

It is used in roadworks, excavation areas and lane closures. The red-and-white colour scheme is standard, with reflective bands on the surface.

Thanks to interlocking connections at the ends, units can be joined side by side to form a continuous line. This creates a much stronger sense of a boundary than individually standing cones.

Some models have a delineator socket, sign holder or solar flasher lamp mount on top. These increase night-time visibility.

## Where is a metal pedestrian barricade used?

It is used for crowd management: police and municipal checkpoints, concert and match venues, rallies, festivals, shopping mall events and queue management.

Typical dimensions are 110 cm high and 200-250 cm wide. The foot measures about 48 cm. Electrostatic powder coating or galvanising is applied — this coating is critical against rust outdoors.

Metal barricades are linked together with hooks, and long lines can be set up. The panel area on the body can be used for promotion and information.

The difference from a plastic barrier: a metal barricade stops people, while a plastic barrier guides vehicles.

## Why choose a water-filled barrier?

It is manufactured hollow and filled with water on site. Because it weighs 10-15 kg when empty, it is easy to transport; when full it rises to 200-250 kg and does not move.

Its main advantage is **impact absorption**. When a vehicle hits it, the flexible body dissipates the kinetic energy. Compared with a concrete barrier, it causes far less damage to both the vehicle and the driver.

It is preferred for long-term roadworks, motorway lane separation and high-speed roadsides.

It needs attention in winter: if there is a risk of freezing, antifreeze should be added to the water or sand should be used.

## When does a folding barrier make sense?

It is designed for applications that are set up and packed away frequently. When folded it takes up little space, and many fit in one vehicle.

It usually carries a warning sign — such as no entry, roadworks or two-way traffic. There are also models where the sign can be attached separately.

Municipal crews, electricity and natural gas maintenance teams and all units carrying out short-term interventions prefer this type.

## What questions should you ask when choosing a barrier?

**How long will it stay?** A few hours: folding; days: plastic; weeks: water-filled.

**What will it stop?** People: metal barricade; guiding vehicles: plastic barrier; physically stopping vehicles: water-filled barrier or concrete.

**Will it be used at night?** Reflective bands are mandatory, and a solar flasher lamp should preferably be added.

**How many metres of line are needed?** Barrier lengths are usually between 100 and 250 cm. Divide the total distance by the barrier length to get the quantity.

**How will it be transported?** Water-filled barriers are transported empty, metal barricades are stacked and folding barriers fold flat. Plastic barriers take up the most space.

## How many barriers do you need?

Formula: **Quantity = Distance to be closed ÷ Barrier length**

For a 100-metre line using 150 cm barriers, 67 units are needed. Extra units should be allowed for ends and turning points.

No gaps are left between barriers — interlocking connections are set up in full contact. If gaps are left, pedestrians pass through and the barrier's purpose is defeated.
