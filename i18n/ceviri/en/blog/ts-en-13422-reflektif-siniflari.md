The specification says "TS EN 13422+A1 certificate of conformity" and "class RA2 reflective". What these two phrases measure, when they are required and what happens if you don't comply — this article explains.

## What is TS EN 13422?

TS EN 13422 is the standard covering portable and deformable traffic warning devices. Traffic cones, cylinders and similar temporary marking products fall within its scope.

The standard defines three main things:

**Physical strength.** The product withstands an impact test at a given speed and continues to function afterwards.

**Resistance to tipping.** It stays in place under wind load and in the draft created by passing vehicles.

**Visibility.** Daytime colour coordinates and night-time retroreflective performance.

The "+A1" suffix indicates an amendment added to the standard later. If the specification says "+A1", compliance with the current version is required.

## What do RA1 and RA2 classes mean?

The performance of a reflective material is measured by its **coefficient of retroreflection**. Its unit is cd/lx/m² — i.e. how much of the light arriving from a given angle is returned to the source.

The EN 12899 standard classifies according to this value:

| Class | Typical structure | Relative brightness | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| RA1 | Glass bead (microsphere) | Reference | Urban, lit road |
| RA2 | Microprismatic | ~3 times RA1 | Motorway, unlit road |
| RA3 | High-performance prismatic | Above RA2 | Special applications |

In practice, the difference means this: under the same headlights, a cone with RA2 bands is noticed from significantly farther away than one with RA1 bands. At high speed, this difference in distance is critical for covering the braking distance.

## When is RA2 required?

RA2 is required under these conditions:

- Roads with a speed limit of 70 km/h and above
- Routes without street lighting
- Night works
- Regions with a high risk of fog and precipitation
- Most public tenders

RA1 is sufficient under these conditions:

- Lit urban roads
- Car park and on-site use
- Mainly daytime applications
- Indoor areas

RA2 material is roughly two to three times more expensive than RA1. Using RA2 where it isn't needed strains the budget unnecessarily; using RA1 where RA2 is needed breaches the specification.

## Do reflective bands lose performance over time?

Yes. Three factors play a role:

**UV exposure.** Sunlight gradually degrades the prismatic structure and the adhesive. Quality bands retain their performance for 5-7 years; cheap ones show a marked decline within 1-2 years.

**Soiling.** Dust and soot layers directly reduce retroreflection. Regular cleaning restores much of the performance.

**Physical damage.** Scratches and tears damage the prismatic structure. A damaged band does not reflect in that area.

A simple field check: at night, shine headlights at it from 50 metres. If the band doesn't shine clearly, it's time to replace it.

## What happens if you buy uncertified products?

There is no legal obligation for private-sector use. However, in the event of an occupational accident, the use of non-standard equipment may create liability during occupational safety inspections.

In public procurement, missing documents have direct consequences. The inspection and acceptance committee rejects the product, the delivery is not accepted, and it can go as far as termination of the contract.

The certificate comes from the manufacturer, not the seller. Ask your supplier for the manufacturer's certificate of conformity for the product.

## What should you look for when reading a specification?

Traffic cone specifications typically state the following:

1. **Height** — 50, 70, 75, 90 cm
2. **Base size and shape** — square, octagonal, hexagonal
3. **Weight** — body weight and base weight (if any) separately
4. **Material** — PVC, PPC, PE
5. **Number and width of reflective bands** — single/double, how many mm
6. **Reflective class** — RA1 or RA2
7. **Colour** — fluorescent orange; a RAL code may be specified
8. **Standard** — TS EN 13422+A1

A deviation in even one of these eight items can eliminate a bid. Mark up the specification item by item and compare it with the product features.

If anything is unclear, you have the right to request a written clarification before the tender date — Public Procurement Law No. 4734 provides for this.
