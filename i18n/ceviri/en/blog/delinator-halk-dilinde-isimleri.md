Eight different names are used in Turkish for the same product. This leads to the wrong product being delivered. This article clarifies the terms.

## What is a delineator?

"Delinatör" comes from the English word *delineator* — meaning "something that marks a boundary". Its technical definition is:

A vertical warning element made from flexible material, fixed to the ground, that bends when hit by a vehicle and returns to its original form.

Three features define it: **flexibility**, **fixing to the ground**, **vertical form**.

## Everyday names

On site and in the market, it goes by these names:

- **Duba** — the most common usage, but a general term
- **Hacıyatmaz** / **hacıyatmaz duba** ("roly-poly") — from its ability to tip over and right itself
- **Plastik duba** ("plastic duba") — emphasises the material
- **Trafik dubası** ("traffic duba") — emphasises the area of use
- **Esnek duba** ("flexible duba") — the most accurate everyday term
- **Trafik babası** ("traffic bollard") — incorrect usage; a bollard is a different product
- **Refüj dubası** ("traffic island duba") — emphasises the place of use
- **Turuncu duba** / **turuncu dikme** ("orange duba / orange post") — emphasises the colour
- **Yol kenar dikmesi** ("roadside post") — emphasises the location
- **Şerit ayırıcı plastik** ("plastic lane separator") — emphasises the function

Most of these names are understandable and acceptable. The problem is the term "baba".

## Are duba, cone and delineator the same thing?

No. "Duba" is an umbrella term used to cover three different products at once.

| Term | Technical equivalent | Fixed to ground? | Form |
|---|---|---|---|
| Delineator | Flexible post | Yes, with wall plugs | Cylindrical, vertical |
| Traffic cone | Cone | No | Conical, with base |
| Baba (bollard) | Fixed barrier | Yes, embedded or flanged | Thick, rigid |
| Refüj dubası | Traffic island nose element | Yes | Wide, broad-based |

Saying "I want a duba" is not enough — which one you mean must be made clear.

## Bollards and delineators get confused

A **baba** (*bollard* in English) is a rigid element designed to form a physical barrier. It is usually made of steel or cast iron and embedded in concrete.

Its purpose is to **stop** the vehicle — it physically blocks passage.

A **delineator**, on the other hand, **warns** the vehicle. It bends when hit, does not stop the vehicle and does not damage it.

This difference is critical. If a delineator is used to keep vehicles out of a pedestrian area, the goal is not achieved — the vehicle simply drives over it. A bollard is needed in that case.

Conversely, if a bollard is used for lane separation, a vehicle that hits it is seriously damaged and there is a risk of injury. A delineator is needed there.

## What is a decorative bollard?

These are aesthetic bollards used in pedestrian areas, historic districts and corporate campuses. They are usually made of cast or composite material in classic forms.

Their function is both to block and to enhance the streetscape. Colour options are offered — such as black, green, navy and gold.

## What should you specify when ordering?

To avoid confusion, provide four pieces of information:

1. **Product type** — delineator / cone / bollard
2. **Height** — 30, 45, 50, 75, 100 cm
3. **Material** — TPU, TPE, PE, PVC
4. **Mounting** — fixed with wall plugs / portable with weighted base

If you also specify the number of reflective bands and the colour, the product is fully defined.

Example of a correct order: *"75 cm TPU delineator, three reflective bands, wall-plug mounting, orange — 40 units."*

## Why does the product code matter?

Every product has a manufacturer code. When the code is given, the naming confusion disappears entirely.

Ordering with a code from the catalogue is the safest way. It also guarantees you get the same product when you reorder — a year later, instead of saying "the same as last time", quoting the code is enough.
