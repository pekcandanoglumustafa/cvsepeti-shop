In indoor car parks, two things get damaged most often: the vehicle's bumper and the corner of the column. Both happen at the same moment, and both can be prevented with the same product.

## What does a column corner protector do?

It provides protection in two directions. If a vehicle hits the column, it absorbs the impact, reducing both cracking of the concrete and scratching of the vehicle.

Its second, less well-known function is visibility. The model with yellow-black diagonal stripes makes the corner stand out; the driver judges the distance better and in most cases the impact never happens.

## Which material?

**Rubber (EPDM or recycled rubber)** — heavy, durable, high impact-absorption capacity. Preferred in high-traffic car parks and commercial facilities. Long-lasting.

**EVA foam** — light, economical, easy to install. Its impact absorption is lower than rubber but sufficient for light contact. Suitable for residential complex and apartment car parks.

**Plastic profile (PVC)** — rigid, does not absorb impact, provides only a visual warning and surface protection. The most economical option.

If you want to protect the column itself, choose rubber; if protecting the vehicle from scratches is enough, choose EVA.

## Where and how is it installed?

**Height:** starts **20-30 cm** above the floor and, with a 90 cm profile, reaches up to 110-120 cm.

The reason for this range is the height of vehicle bumpers. Taking it all the way down to the floor is unnecessary — the part near the floor gets damaged during sweeping and washing.

**Installation method:** there are two options.

*Adhesive* — bonded directly to the concrete with polyurethane or construction adhesive. No drilling, quick, and sufficient for most applications.

*Wall plugs* — fixed with wall plugs and screws through the holes in the profile. Preferred for heavy rubber profiles and points that take frequent impacts.

Before installation, the concrete surface should be cleaned and dust and loose particles removed.

## How many do you need?

The **two corners** of a column facing traffic are protected. If all four corners are exposed to traffic, four units are needed.

Calculation examples:
- Car park with 8 columns, 2 corners per column → **16 units**
- If wall corners are also to be protected, add 1 unit per corner
- Columns at ramp entrances take the most impacts — give them priority

In addition, **wall protection strips** can be used along walls. These are for flat surfaces rather than corners and protect vehicle doors.

## Does it need to be reflective?

Lighting in indoor car parks is usually weak. A model with reflective stripes makes the corner stand out and prevents the impact in the first place.

A plain black protector absorbs impact but is not visible — it protects, but it does not prevent the accident.

Yellow-black diagonal stripes are the standard warning pattern and are universally recognised. White-red is also used, but yellow-black is more common in car parks.

## Should column protectors and wheel stops be used together?

They do different jobs and are usually used together.

A **wheel stop** stops the vehicle at a set distance — ensuring it never gets close to the column.

A **column protector** comes into play when the wheel stop does not help: if the driver goes over the stop, passes to the side of it or turns while manoeuvring.

A wheel stop alone is not enough because most damage happens during reversing and tight turns — there the vehicle touches the column, not the stop.

## How long does it last?

A rubber profile lasts 5-10 years with correct installation. EVA foam lasts 3-5 years.

Situations requiring replacement: the profile coming loose, the reflective stripe being completely worn away, deep tears forming.

An annual check is sufficient. For adhesive-mounted protectors, if the edges have started to lift, act early — once it falls off completely, there is no protection.
