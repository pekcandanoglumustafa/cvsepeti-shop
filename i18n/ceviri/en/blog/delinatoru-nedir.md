A delineator is a flexible post that guides drivers and marks a boundary without creating a physical obstacle. It is designed not to cause damage on impact — it bends when a vehicle touches it and straightens up after it passes.

## Where is a delineator used?

There are five main areas of use:

- **Lane separation** — separating opposing traffic, preventing overtaking
- **Traffic island noses** — marking the start of junction and turning islands
- **Car park layout** — separating parking bays, guiding traffic flow
- **Around schools and hospitals** — pedestrian safety, slowing vehicles
- **Construction site boundaries** — enclosing the work zone

The purpose of a delineator is to warn, not to block. Where a physical barrier is needed, a safety barrier should be used instead of a delineator.

## How do you choose the delineator height?

Height is determined by the driver's line of sight and the intended use.

| Height | Use |
|---|---|
| 30 cm | Car parks, pedestrian area boundaries |
| 45–50 cm | Urban lane separation, island noses |
| 70–75 cm | Main arterials, heavy traffic |
| 100 cm | Motorways, long-distance visibility |

A taller delineator is visible from further away, but its wind load also increases. If a 100 cm delineator is to be used in an open area, the base fixing must be strong.

## What is the difference between TPU, TPE and PE?

The three materials behave differently:

**TPU (thermoplastic polyurethane)** is the most durable. It stays flexible in the cold, withstands many impacts and is little affected by UV light. It is preferred for long-term use.

**TPE (thermoplastic elastomer)** is mid-range. It offers flexibility close to TPU at a lower cost.

**PE (polyethylene)** is the most economical. It is sufficient in mild climates and in places with low impact risk. It can become brittle in the cold.

Because winter temperatures are low in inland regions of Türkiye, TPU or TPE is recommended. On the coast, PE can also be used.

## How is a delineator installed?

There are two methods:

**Wall-plug installation** — a hole is drilled in the asphalt or concrete, a plug is driven in and the delineator is fixed with screws. This is the standard for permanent applications. Ø10×110 mm lag screws and Ø16×90 mm plastic wall plugs are generally used.

**Adhesive installation** — fixed to the ground with polyurethane-based adhesive. Preferred on surfaces that cannot be drilled; its strength is lower than wall-plug installation.

For temporary use, there are portable models with weighted bases — these are not fixed and stay in place by their own weight.

## What does the number of reflective bands mean?

The number of reflective bands on a delineator indicates its visibility level. 30 cm models usually have one band, 45-50 cm models two bands, 70-75 cm models three bands and 100 cm models four bands.

The number of bands increases in proportion to height because the goal is for the post to be visible along its entire height. A tall delineator with a band only at the top distorts distance perception because its lower part stays in the dark.
