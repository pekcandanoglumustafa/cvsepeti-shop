The one thing that determines a delineator's service life is its material. Two delineators of the same height and appearance can differ threefold in lifespan — the difference comes from the raw material of the body.

## What is TPU?

TPU stands for thermoplastic polyurethane. It is considered the ideal material for delineators.

Three properties define it:

**Shape memory.** It returns to its original form after impact. Even after hundreds of impacts, permanent deformation is minimal.

**Low-temperature flexibility.** It stays flexible even at minus 20 degrees. It does not break.

**UV resistance.** With additives in production, it does not lose colour or strength after years in the sun.

It has the highest cost but the lowest cost over its lifetime.

## What is TPE and how does it differ from TPU?

TPE stands for thermoplastic elastomer. It sits between TPU and PE.

Its flexibility is close to TPU, but its shape memory is somewhat weaker under repeated impacts. Its cold performance is lower than TPU and higher than PE.

It costs about 15-20% less than TPU. It is a reasonable balance for medium-density traffic and mild climates.

## When is a PE delineator sufficient?

PE stands for polyethylene. It is the most economical option.

It is sufficient under these conditions:

- Indoor car parks and interiors
- Areas with low impact risk
- Mild climates, regions where winter temperatures do not drop far below zero
- Short-term or temporary applications
- Situations where budget is critical and the product is replaced annually

It is not suitable under these conditions:

- Cold regions — it becomes brittle
- Points with frequent vehicle contact — it deforms permanently
- Long-term permanent installations

## Comparison table

| | TPU | TPE | PE |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flexibility | Very high | High | Medium |
| Shape memory | Excellent | Good | Poor |
| Cold resistance | Down to −20 °C | Down to −10 °C | Limited |
| UV resistance | High | Medium-high | Medium |
| Typical life | 4–6 years | 3–4 years | 1.5–2 years |
| Relative price | Reference | 15–20% lower | 30–40% lower |

Lifespan values are given for medium-density vehicle contact. In areas without impacts, all three materials last longer.

## Which one for conditions in Türkiye?

Climate is the decisive factor.

**Central and Eastern Anatolia** — Konya, Ankara, Kayseri, Erzurum, Sivas. Winter temperatures drop to minus 15-20 degrees. PE becomes brittle under these conditions and breaks at the first impact. **TPU or TPE should be chosen.**

**Mediterranean and Aegean coast** — Antalya, İzmir, Muğla. Winters are mild and frost is limited. PE can be used. However, because the summer sun is intense, the UV additive is important.

**Black Sea** — High humidity, mild temperatures. PE can be used; UV resistance is less critical.

**Marmara** — An intermediate region. For medium-density use, TPE is a balanced choice.

## How can you tell the material?

It is hard to distinguish visually. There are three ways to check:

**Product description.** Reputable sellers state the raw material. If it is not stated, that is a problem.

**Flexibility test.** Bend the delineator over by hand. TPU and TPE show soft resistance and straighten slowly when released. PE feels stiffer and either springs back quickly or stays at an angle.

**Cold test.** Leave a sample in the refrigerator for a few hours and then bend it. PE stiffens noticeably under these conditions.

## What material are economy models made of?

The "eco" label usually indicates one of two things: thinner wall thickness or a more economical raw material.

Economy models are not bad — used in the right place, they lower costs. In an indoor car park or along a boundary with no impact risk, an economy model will last for years without problems.

However, on a main arterial or at a point with constant vehicle contact, an economy model soon generates replacement costs. The decision should be based on where it will be used.
