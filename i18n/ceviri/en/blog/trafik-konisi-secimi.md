Choosing a traffic cone is a more technical decision than it looks. Choose the wrong height and drivers notice the cone too late; choose too little weight and it tips over at the first gust of wind. This guide explains the three main criteria and which one is needed in which situation.

## How tall should a traffic cone be?

The area of use determines the height. For short-term urban works, 50 cm is sufficient. On main arterials and roads with a speed limit above 50 km/h, 75 cm is needed. On motorways, for night works and where there is heavy vehicle traffic, 90 cm cones should be used.

The logic is simple: as the driver's speed increases, the detection distance gets longer, and the cone needs to be visible from further away. Height directly affects visibility.

| Height | Area of use | Typical speed |
|---|---|---|
| 30–50 cm | Car parks, warehouses, short urban jobs | 0–30 km/h |
| 70–75 cm | Main streets, around construction sites | 30–70 km/h |
| 90 cm | Motorways, night works, heavy vehicles | 70+ km/h |

## When is a weighted-base cone needed?

Weighted-base models are used in areas exposed to wind, on bridges, on roads with heavy vehicle traffic and in works where cones will stay in place for a long time. The weight base is usually made of recycled rubber and slips over the base of the cone.

A standard 75 cm PVC cone weighs about 3 kg. The weighted-base version of the same cone weighs 6-7 kg. This difference determines its resistance to the air current created by passing vehicles.

Indoors, in enclosed areas or for short-term use, a weight base is unnecessary — it makes handling harder and increases cost.

## Why does the number of reflective bands matter?

Reflective tape sends light from headlights back in the direction it came from. The more bands, the greater the cone's night-time visibility.

- **Non-reflective**: daytime use only, enclosed areas, warehouses
- **Single reflective**: urban areas, lit roads
- **Double reflective**: night works, unlit roads, motorways

On a double-reflective cone, the lower band is seen at close range and the upper band at long range. That is why double bands are standard on high-speed roads.

The quality of the reflective material also makes a difference. Microprismatic tapes are visible from further away than glass bead tapes.

## PVC cone or PE cone?

PVC cones are more flexible, regain their shape when hit by a vehicle and last longer. PE cones are lighter and cheaper but become brittle in the cold.

For continuous use where vehicle contact is likely, PVC should be chosen. For short-term use in controlled areas, PE is sufficient.

## How many cones do you need?

In highway applications, the spacing between cones is determined by speed. General rule: leave a gap in metres equal to half the speed limit. 25 metres at 50 km/h, 45 metres at 90 km/h.

Lane closures require additional cones for the transition zone. Typically 15-20 cones are used for a 100-metre lane closure.

## Which standards should a traffic cone comply with?

In Türkiye, traffic cones are subject to the technical specifications of the General Directorate of Highways (KGM). The EN 12899 and TS EN 13422 standards are the reference for reflective material. In public tenders, a certificate of compliance with these standards is required.

There is no obligation for private sector use, but non-standard products can cause problems in occupational safety inspections.
