Both products are orange, both have reflective bands, both warn drivers. But when one is used in place of the other, either money is wasted or the job does not get done.

What is the basic difference between a delineator and a cone?

The difference in one word: fixing.

A traffic cone is not fixed to the ground. It stays in place by its own weight, is lifted by hand, stacked and loaded onto a truck. Its design is built around portability.

A delineator is screwed to the ground with wall plugs. Removing it requires tools. Its design is built around staying in place — it bends when hit, then stands back up.

When should a cone be used?

A cone is the right choice for:

  • Short-term roadworks — a one-day asphalt patch, road marking paint
  • Accidents and emergencies — traffic diversion, incident scene safety
  • Events and organisations — temporary parking layouts, walking routes
  • Lane narrowing — temporary lane closure during maintenance work
  • Warehouses and sites — constantly changing layouts

The cone's greatest advantage is flexibility. You can set up a layout in the morning and pack it away in the evening. Because 90 cm cones nest inside each other, 20 of them fit in the back of a van.

When should a delineator be used?

A delineator is the right choice for:

  • Permanent lane separation — separating opposing traffic, preventing overtaking
  • Traffic island nose marking — the start of junction islands
  • Car park layout — permanent boundaries between parking bays
  • Around schools and hospitals — permanent installations for pedestrian safety
  • Restricted area boundaries — enclosing areas that must not be entered

The delineator's advantage is permanence. It is installed once and requires no maintenance for years. Even when hit by a vehicle, it does not come loose; it bends and straightens back up.

Is there a middle ground? The lane separator

Yes. The delineator lane separator sits between the two.

A delineator is mounted on a rubber or PVC base. The base can be screwed to the ground, but it also stays in place by its own weight without screws. This gives a solution that is both permanent and semi-portable.

This product is used especially for seasonal layouts, periodically changing traffic flows and surfaces that cannot be drilled.

The base also prevents vehicle wheels from hitting the delineator directly, extending its life.

Can permanent separation be done with cones?

Technically yes, but three problems arise.

Shifting. The air current created by passing heavy vehicles blows cones around. A weighted-base model reduces this but does not eliminate it. Realignment labour is needed several times a week.

Theft and loss. Unfixed cones go missing. In permanent use, the annual loss rate is high.

Visual disorder. Over time cones scatter and their alignment is lost. In a corporate setting this gives a neglected impression.

When these three costs are added up, the installation cost of a delineator is quickly recovered.

Can a delineator be used for temporary applications?

It can, but it is unnecessary. Installing a delineator requires drilling into the ground — drilling into asphalt for a one-day job is both costly and causes permanent damage to the surface.

For temporary use, portable delineator models with weighted bases can be used. These are not fixed and stay in place by their own weight. In that case, however, you lose the cone's transport advantage — delineators do not nest and are hard to transport.

Decision table

Ask this question: will this product still be in the same place three months from now?

If yes, a delineator. If no, a cone. If maybe, a delineator lane separator.
