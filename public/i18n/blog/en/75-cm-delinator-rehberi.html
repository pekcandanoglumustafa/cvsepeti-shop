75 cm is the most balanced of the delineator heights. 50 cm is enough for urban streets but is too low on main arterials; 100 cm offers high visibility but increases wind load and cost. 75 cm sits right between the two.

Technical specifications of the 75 cm delineator

The standard model on the market has the following specifications:

In economy models the weight drops to 750-850 grams and the wall thickness is thinner. This directly affects impact resistance.

Where is it used?

Lane separation on main arterials — separating opposing traffic and preventing overtaking. This is the most common use.

Traffic island noses — marking the start of junction islands. The first three delineators are placed more densely.

Bus and bicycle lanes — physically separating dedicated lanes.

Around schools and hospitals — narrowing the roadway for pedestrian safety.

Around construction sites — marking the work zone boundary.

A 50 cm delineator is too low for these applications; drivers notice it too late at high speed. 75 cm is the right choice for the 50-70 km/h speed range.

How many do you need?

Lane separation uses 3-6 metre spacing. The higher the speed, the wider the spacing.

Quantity = (Distance ÷ Spacing) + 1

Examples:
- 100 m main arterial, 4 m spacing → 26 units
- 200 m ring road, 6 m spacing → 34 units
- 60 m school zone, 3 m spacing → 21 units

Allow an extra 10-15% for denser spacing at junctions and turning points. It is also sensible to stock spares equal to 5% of the total for the first year — vehicle contact is inevitable.

What is the carton advantage?

The manufacturer's carton holds 10 units. Ordering in multiples of 10 lowers shipping costs.

Here is why: the carton volume is fixed and takes up the same space whether full or half full. 10 units ship as one carton, whereas 12 units means one carton plus a separate package.

The actual shipping volume of 15 units of the 45 cm delineator is 44 desi. 20 units (2 full cartons) ship more efficiently.

For bulk purchases, ordering in full-carton multiples such as 10, 20 or 30 brings advantages both in shipping and in manufacturer discounts.

TPU or economy model?

The TPU model weighs 950 g, the economy model 750-850 g. The difference comes from wall thickness and raw material quality.

Choose TPU for: main arterials, points with constant vehicle contact, cold regions, long-term installations.

Economy is enough for: indoor car parks, low-impact boundaries, mild climates, short-term installations.

In provinces with harsh winters such as Konya, Ankara and Erzurum, the economy model becomes brittle. The price difference is recovered with the first broken unit.

How is it installed?

It is fixed at three points. Drill a hole 90-100 mm deep with a Ø16 mm bit, clean the hole, drive in a Ø16×90 mm plastic wall plug and tighten a Ø10×110 mm lag screw with a socket wrench.

Critical point: the drill bit must be exactly 16 mm. With a smaller bit the plug will not go in; with a larger bit there is play and the screw will not hold.

Installing one delineator on asphalt takes 8-10 minutes on average. A 100-unit job can be finished by two people in one day.

Why three reflective bands?

The number of bands increases with height because the goal is for the full height of the post to be visible.

A tall delineator with a band only at the top creates a false sense of distance at night — the driver cannot see where the post ends. Three bands make the line clear at both short and long range.

The number of bands is decisive in product selection: a "75 cm with triple reflective" and a "75 cm with single reflective" are different products and are specified separately in technical specifications.
