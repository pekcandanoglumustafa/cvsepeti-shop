A road stud is a small part, but in night driving it does more work than the lane line. In the rain the painted line disappears; the stud stays visible.

What is a road stud?

It is a marking element fixed to the asphalt that reflects light from headlights back towards the driver. It is commonly known as a cat's eye.

The body can be die-cast aluminium, ABS plastic or polycarbonate. The reflector inside has a prismatic structure and sends light back in the direction it came from.

Glass bead vs lens road studs

There are two technologies, and the difference in performance is large.

Glass bead — small glass beads sit on the surface. It is cheap, offers medium reflectivity and wears over time.

Lens (prismatic) — it contains a cube-corner prism. It returns light far more efficiently, is visible 3-5 times farther away than the glass bead type and resists wear.

On high-speed roads and unlit routes, the lens type should be preferred.

What does the colour code mean?

The colour of road studs is not chosen at random; it carries meaning.

ColourMeaning
WhiteNormal lane separation
YellowCentral median, separation of opposing traffic
RedWrong-way warning, road edge
GreenEmergency exit, escape route
BlueFire hydrant location

There are also bi-colour studs — white from one direction and red from the other. On one-way roads, these warn drivers who enter from the wrong direction.

How are they installed?

There are three methods:

Bonded — fixed to the asphalt with epoxy or polyurethane adhesive. This is the fastest method and is sufficient for most applications. The surface must be clean and dry.

Shank-mounted — holes are drilled for the shanks under the stud, which is then set in place together with adhesive. Preferred in areas where snowploughs operate, as the risk of being torn out is low.

Recessed — the asphalt is milled and the stud is set flush with the surface. This is the most durable method, and snowplough blades don't catch on the stud. It also involves the most labour.

Before installation, the surface should be swept and cleared of oil and dust. A stud bonded to a dirty surface comes loose in the first winter.

How far apart are they placed?

Spacing depends on the road and the purpose.

LocationSpacing
Straight lane separation9–12 m
Approach to bends and junctions3–6 m
Before a pedestrian crossing2–3 m
Median nose1–2 m
Car park lane separation2–4 m

The rule is this: the more critical the information the driver must perceive, the tighter the spacing. On a straight road, sparse placement is enough; on a bend, tighter spacing signals the change of direction in advance.

How does it differ from a floor marking stud?

A road stud forms a line and provides direction and lane information.

A floor marking stud (dot stud), on the other hand, marks an area — parking space boundaries, pedestrian areas, warning zones. It is usually larger and flatter and can be walked on.

Tactile surface elements for the visually impaired are a separate category as well: the raised dots and lines are designed to be detected with a cane and underfoot, and their reflective function is secondary.

How long do they last?

A correctly installed road stud lasts 3-7 years. Three factors determine its service life:

Vehicle contact — studs on the lane are constantly run over. Positioning them off the lane, at the edge of the line, extends their life.

Snow ploughing — as the blade passes over the surface, it rips out bonded studs. In snowy regions, recessed installation should be preferred.

Soiling — soot and dust building up on the surface reduce reflectivity. Rain cleans them to some extent, but in dirty areas performance declines over time.

A night check should be carried out once a year: if the studs don't shine clearly when viewed with headlights from 50-100 metres, it's time to replace them.
