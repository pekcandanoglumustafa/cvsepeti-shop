Night visibility starts where reflective tape is not enough. Reflective material only works when light falls on it — a point outside the headlight beam is invisible. An active light source fills this gap.

What is a solar flasher lamp?

It is an LED warning lamp that charges during the day via the solar panel on top and switches on automatically in the dark via a light sensor.

No cables are run, no socket is needed and no batteries are changed. All you need to do is mount it in a spot that gets sunlight.

What does lens diameter mean?

Lens diameter is the main measure that determines visibility distance.

Lens diameterVisibilityUse
100 mm400–600 mUrban areas, construction sites, car parks
200 mm800–1000 mMain roads, motorways, junctions

A 200 mm lens is both larger and contains more LEDs. It should be chosen for unlit roads and high-speed routes.

How long does the battery last?

A fully charged lamp usually runs continuously for 60-100 hours. That means 4-6 days without sunlight.

In winter months and in persistently overcast weather, charging takes longer. For critical applications, it makes sense to keep spare lamps or choose models that can be briefly charged with an adapter.

Battery life is usually 2-3 years. Models with replaceable batteries are more economical in the long run.

Steady light or flashing?

There are two modes, and they do different jobs.

Flashing — to attract attention. The eye detects movement, so it is far more noticeable than a steady light. Used at hazard points and in temporary applications.

Steady light — to mark a boundary. When marking a line, flashing is confusing; a steady light gives the impression of a continuous line.

Some models support both, switched with a button.

Colour choice also carries meaning: yellow/amber for general warning, red for danger and stopping, blue for official vehicles and checkpoints.

Where is it mounted?

On top of barriers — most plastic barriers have a flasher mount.

On top of delineators and posts — fitted directly to models with a socket.

On top of cones — mounted on the cone handle or on the tip with an adapter.

Traffic island noses — to mark the start of the island at night.

On its own stand — models with a base plate are placed on or screwed to the ground.

When choosing the mounting point, sunlight is critical. A lamp placed under a tree, under eaves or in a shaded north-facing spot will not charge.

How many do you need?

Flasher lamps are not placed at every point — they go at critical points that need to attract attention.

A typical layout for a lane closure:
- 1 unit at the start of the transition zone
- 1 unit at the narrowest point of the taper
- 1 unit at the end of the work zone

For a 100-metre installation, 3-5 lamps are sufficient. Fitting a lamp to every cone is both an unnecessary cost and creates visual clutter.

Why does the IP protection rating matter?

Water and dust protection is essential for an outdoor product. IP54 withstands rain; IP65 withstands strong water jets.

A lamp for roadside use should be at least IP54. Muddy water splashed by passing vehicles quickly ruins a lamp without IP protection.

Solar or battery-powered?

Battery-powered lamps are cheaper and work in places without sunlight. However, battery replacement requires ongoing attention — changing the batteries of 50 lamps every three months is serious labour.

The solar model is more expensive as an initial investment, but its operating cost is close to zero. In continuous use, it pays for itself in the second year.

Decision rule: temporary, short-term jobs — battery; permanent, long-term jobs — solar.
