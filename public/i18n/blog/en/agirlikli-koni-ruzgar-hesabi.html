A cone that falls over is useless. Worse, it is harmful — it gets blown into the road, confuses drivers and ends up under the next vehicle. That is why the weight decision is not a visual preference but a technical calculation.

Why do cones tip over?

Two forces are at work:

Natural wind. In open areas, a 40-50 km/h wind can knock down a cone with a large surface. The taller the cone, the larger the surface area and the overturning moment.

Vehicle air currents. This one is more often overlooked. A passing lorry leaves a short but strong air current behind it. A truck passing at 90 km/h pushes the cone next to it with more force than the wind.

The second effect is especially important because cones can tip over even on a windless day.

The weight base is usually made of recycled rubber and slips over the base of the cone. Hexagonal bases are common in 38 cm and 48 cm diameters.

When do you need a weighted model?

Choose a weighted model if two of the following conditions apply at the same time:

  • Open area, location exposed to wind
  • Road with a speed limit above 70 km/h
  • Heavy vehicle traffic
  • Bridge, viaduct or elevated location
  • The cone will stay in place for more than a day
  • Coastal area or constantly windy geography

If only one condition applies, the standard model is usually sufficient.

When is it not needed?

  • Indoor car parks and interiors
  • Around buildings, sheltered areas
  • Speed limit below 30 km/h
  • Supervised use for a few hours
  • Use that requires frequent relocation

Unnecessary weight brings three costs: purchase price, handling labour and shipping desi.

Alternative: can the weight ring be bought separately?

Yes. The weight base is sold as a separate product and can be fitted to an existing cone.

This allows a flexible strategy: instead of buying all your cones weighted, you buy standard cones and add weight rings where they are needed.

For example, if 20 of 50 cones will be used in an open area and 30 in an indoor car park: buying 50 standard cones + 20 weight rings is cheaper than buying 50 weighted cones.

The rings can be moved to other cones when needs change.

Does the base shape affect stability?

Yes. The wider the base and the lower the centre of gravity, the better the stability.

Square base — large footprint, good stability, takes up little space when stacked.

Octagonal base — stability close to square, more attractive look, common in production.

Hexagonal weight ring — lowest centre of gravity, best stability.

Round base — advantageous for stacking but lower resistance to tipping than the others.

Can sandbags or concrete be used?

On site, sand is sometimes poured into the cone or a weight is placed on top. These are makeshift solutions and are not recommended.

Sand poured into the cone raises the centre of gravity and upsets the balance. A weight placed on top is equally harmful.

The right weight belongs at the base of the cone. That is why weight rings are designed to fit at base level.

How many weighted cones do you need?

Mixed use is usually the most efficient. In a lane closure:

  • Transition zone (first 30-50 m) — weighted, because it is closest to traffic and the most critical point
  • Middle zone — standard is enough
  • Termination — weighted, the last point the driver sees

With this distribution, total cost goes down while safety is maintained.
